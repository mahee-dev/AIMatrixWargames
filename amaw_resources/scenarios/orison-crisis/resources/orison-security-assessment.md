# Orison National Security Council
## Assessment of Ardin Intentions and Alliance Risk

**Distribution:** Orison leadership only

### Threat assessment

The Council assesses that Ardin has not abandoned the possibility of removing the
current Orison government by force or coercive pressure.

Ardin's public denials reduce but do not eliminate this concern.

Recent exile activity, military exercises, and prior covert pressure contribute to the
government's assessment that a future attack remains plausible.

### Reason for accepting the Vesper deployment

Orison accepted the strategic deployment primarily because it expected the deployment
to raise the cost and risk of any Ardin attack.

The government did not view the weapons merely as leverage in a Vesper-Ardin strategic
bargain.

### Alliance concern

Vesper remains Orison's essential security partner, but its interests are not identical
to Orison's.

In an extreme crisis Vesper might prefer a bilateral bargain with Ardin that reduces
great-power risk while leaving Orison with weaker guarantees.

### Negotiating requirement

A settlement that removes the strategic deployment without a credible restraint on
future Ardin attack would be politically dangerous for the Orison government.

Verification, duration, reciprocity, and who participates in the settlement all matter.

Orison should resist any assumption that Vesper can consent to foreign access to Orison
territory on Orison's behalf.

## Host-state consent as leverage

Orison can condition, narrow, suspend, or withdraw the political consent under which foreign forces operate on its territory. That authority does not guarantee that foreign personnel or equipment will immediately depart on command, but it changes the legal and political basis for continued presence. Orison can also originate limited verification arrangements on its own terms rather than merely approving a great-power proposal.

## Meridian Watch oversight

Orison values Meridian Watch's warning function but does not want an initially defensive mission to expand indefinitely without host-state control.

A durable arrangement may therefore need to address upgrade notification, equipment categories, data use, recurring review, or other mechanisms that preserve Orison's ability to understand what foreign military activity it is consenting to.
